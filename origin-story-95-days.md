# Ninety-five days

*The story behind why we built ATALNT AI. For the site, the deck, and sales conversations.*

---

## The written version

**A commercial construction firm in the Southeast renovates working hospitals.**
Occupied buildings, live wards, infection-control protocols, phased shutdowns. It is
one of the harder things to build, and one of the harder things to staff.

They needed a traveling superintendent. The project had already broken ground, which
meant they didn't need someone in six weeks. They needed someone immediately.

We found him. Healthcare ground-up experience *and* renovation experience — the
combination that actually matters on an occupied hospital, and the reason most
candidates get cut. Every reference checked out. The interviews were green flags all
the way through.

He was productive by his second week.

**Base salary was $130,000. Our fee was 25 percent. The client wired $32,500.**

That is how the contingent model works, and by every measure it worked here. Fast
placement, correct hire, immediate productivity.

### Then, in May, he gave two weeks' notice.

Personal reasons. Nothing anyone could have screened for, and nothing I'd hold against
him — people's lives change.

He resigned on **day ninety-five**.

Our guarantee runs ninety days.

### Five days

Five days decided whether that client was owed a replacement search or owed nothing
at all.

Not the quality of the hire. Not the work he did. Not anything either of us could
control. **Five days on a calendar.**

The client was out $32,500, back to an empty seat on a live hospital project, and
looking at another $32,500 to fill it again. They felt wronged. I don't blame them —
they *were* wronged. Just not by me, and not by him.

They were wronged by a pricing model that turns a man's personal crisis into a
thirty-two thousand dollar problem for somebody else.

### What actually broke

I want to be precise, because this is the part that matters.

ATALNT AI would not have kept that superintendent in the job. No system would have.
He left for reasons that had nothing to do with fit, and no screening process —
human or AI — catches that.

**What ATALNT AI changes is what it costs you when it happens.**

Under a subscription, that client pays a flat monthly fee. When the superintendent
resigns, the replacement search costs them nothing extra. No second $32,500. No
argument about which side of a ninety-day line the resignation landed on. No
guarantee window at all, because a guarantee is a bandage on a model that shouldn't
need one.

A ninety-day guarantee exists because the fee is enormous and one-time. Remove the
one-time fee and the guarantee becomes irrelevant.

### The third option

For most of my career there were two ways to hire. Pay an agency every time. Or build
the whole operation yourself.

I watched a good client pay $32,500 for a hire that lasted three months, and then
discover the thing protecting them had expired five days earlier.

That's the day I stopped trying to sell the model and started trying to replace it.

**That's why we built ATALNT AI.**

departure is what makes the rest believable. A prospect who's been in recruiting knows
no system stops someone leaving for personal reasons. Claiming otherwise loses them.
Claiming only that the *cost* shouldn't compound is a promise you can actually keep.
