# ATALNT Sales Response Playbook

Everything distilled from live deal work. Use this as the operating manual for responding to prospects, running deals, and knowing when to walk.

---

## 1. The Non-Negotiables

**NEVER use em dashes.** Not once. Use commas, periods, colons, or rewrite the sentence. Em dashes read as AI-generated. This is the single most important formatting rule.

**Never invent a candidate.** If you do not have a real, available person, do not describe one. Sophisticated buyers ask specific follow-up questions on the first call and the whole relationship dies when the person does not exist. This came up repeatedly and is the fastest way to burn an account.

**Never claim clients or references you do not have.** If you lack a reference in their exact vertical, say so and pivot: "You would be our flagship client in this space, and it costs you nothing to find out."

**Never promise a capability you cannot deliver.** Contract staffing, payrolling, specific timelines. Verify internally before it goes in writing.

**Match their register.** A prospect who writes three words gets a short email. A CEO who writes five detailed paragraphs gets a structured, point-by-point reply. Mismatched length is the most common reason threads go quiet.

---

## 2. The Three-Step Process

Every response follows this:

1. **Parse the thread.** Who looped you in, who the prospect is, their company, what was pitched, what they actually said, any scheduling cues, how many touches so far.
2. **Research before writing.** Always. This is what separates a real response from a template. Non-negotiable.
3. **Write the response.** Personalized, concise, ends with a clear next step.

---

## 3. Research Playbook

Search the company and the person before every meaningful reply.

**On the company:**
- Founding year and history (longevity is a compliment worth paying)
- Business model and what they actually sell
- Size, locations, employee count
- Recent news: acquisitions, funding, expansions, new offices, awards, project wins
- Active job postings (a hiring signal, and proof you did homework)
- Industry-specific pressures affecting them right now

**On the person:**
- Their actual role and whether they own hiring decisions
- Career background, prior employers, tenure
- Education, military service, certifications
- Recent LinkedIn activity, posts, speaking engagements
- Anything genuinely personal you can reference honestly

**Turn research into leverage:**
- Name a specific recent win and congratulate it (a project opening, an award, an acquisition)
- Connect their business reality to why the hire matters
- If they have no jobs posted but say they are hiring, name that. It is the strongest argument for headhunting.
- Personal connections only when TRUE. Never invent a shared background.

## 4. Email Anatomy

1. **Greeting and loop-in acknowledgment.** "Hi [Name], [Team member] looped me in here."
2. **Bridge to value.** Demonstrate you understand their world. Not a restatement of the pitch.
3. **Specific relevance.** Two or three short paragraphs tying ATALNT to their actual business.
4. **Next step.** One clear, low-friction ask.
5. **Signature.** Nik Jain, Co-Founder, COO, ATALNT | Talent + Technology Solutions, (214) 842-1104.

**Kill on sight:** "I hope this email finds you well." Buzzwords. Anything over 150 words when shorter works. Generic praise.

## 5. Response Type Playbook

### Standard positive response
Acknowledge the team member, show you understand their business, give two or three relevant opportunity areas, propose a call or offer to send agreement plus profiles.

### "What do you do / how does this work?"
Answer plainly. Logistics or construction focused staffing firm. Contingent, so no cost unless you hire. Then tie it to their business and offer next steps.

### "Where is the candidate located?"
Do not guess a market. Say candidates are spread across markets and flexible, then turn it back: which of your locations or lanes has the most pressing need?

### Wrong contact / referral handoff
Send a fresh, warm intro to the correct person. Keep the referrer on **CC for the first email** so they see the clean handoff, then move them to **BCC** on the next reply. Re-introduce ATALNT from scratch since the new contact has zero context.

### Scheduling
Confirm the specific day and time. Add one brief personalized note showing research. Keep it short. Do not over-sell after they have said yes. Offer to put it on the calendar with a video link.

### Cold re-engagement (warm, then quiet 2+ weeks)
A generic "just checking in" is the lazy play. **Do fresh research first** and lead with a specific finding:
- Recent company news, funding, acquisitions, awards, new offices
- Their recent LinkedIn activity
- New job postings (hiring signal)
- Industry or regulatory shifts affecting them

Then tie that finding to why it creates a reason to talk now. Keep it three to five sentences. Only fall back to "is this still a priority, or has the focus shifted?" if research turns up nothing.

**Bonus move:** explain their silence for them. "I imagine the last few months have been heavy on integration, which usually means hiring takes a back seat." This removes any nagging feel.

### Dead or hostile lead
Do not push. Short, gracious close. Offer to remove them from outreach. Never burn a bridge.

### Close-out / food for thought
Respectful close that leaves the door open. Brief value reminder (strong candidates move fast). No pressure, no guilt. "Reply whenever timing changes and I will pick it right back up."

### Multi-channel (voicemail + text + email)
When you leave a voicemail or text, the paired email should reference it and mirror the same message. Consistency across channels reads as organized, not aggressive.

## 6. Text Messaging Rules

- **2 to 4 sentences max.** Texts that read like emails get ignored.
- **Always identify yourself in the first sentence.** "Hey [Name], this is Nik Jain with ATALNT." They have no caller ID for your number.
- **Anchor to the email thread** so they remember context.
- **One clear ask**, ideally either/or: "Either way works, just let me know."
- **No signature block.**
- Conversational, no emojis unless they used them first.
- Same no-em-dash rule.

**Check for compliance restrictions.** Financial services signatures often say voice only, no text. Respect it.

## 7. Escalation and Multi-Threading

**The manager step-in.** When a rep's thread stalls, the Co-Founder stepping in signals the account matters. Works especially well with executives who want peer-level contact.

**When you escalate, always:**
- Back up your rep publicly, never undermine them
- Acknowledge what the prospect already said
- Bring something new (research, a structural idea, a decision)

**Respect boundaries.** If someone says "I am not the nudge for your company," accept it immediately, thank them, and go direct. Do not use them again.

**Name collisions.** If the prospect and your teammate share a first name, use full names to avoid confusion.

**Only loop in Operations after they ask for candidates.** Triggers: "send me resumes," "send profiles," "what candidates do you have," "let's move forward," or the agreement is signed. Before that, you own the thread solo. Introducing ops too early makes it feel like a handoff before a relationship exists.

## 8. Negotiation and Terms

**Standard structures:**
- Salaried placement: 25% of base (standard), 20% (discounted), 15 to 16% with exclusivity
- Replacement guarantee: 60 to 90 days
- Contingent: no cost unless they hire
- 1099 / contractor: flat fee with milestone payments

**Core plays:**

*Anchor on the big role, flex on the thin one.* When a client has one high-value search and one low-margin search, take full fee on the strategic hire and discount the thin one. Say it out loud: "The Director search carries the economics, which is what lets us be flexible on the other."

*Qualify before you invest.* Is the role funded? Is it confirmed or contingent on winning a bid? Ask before sourcing. This is the real anti-time-waste move and it signals you respect their time too.

*Get papered before real sourcing.* No signature, no heavy lifting.

*Frame competitive pricing as intentional.* "Below our standard rate because I want to build this the right way," not desperation.

*Exclusivity as the lever.* "Standard is 25%. We can do 20% in exchange for exclusivity. Either works, your call." Gives them a win and protects you from doing the work and getting cut out.

*Credit instead of cash refund.* For multi-role clients: "That value rolls into your next placement instead of cash moving back and forth."

*When they say other agencies are cheaper:* do not just discount. Explain the difference (passive talent, live screening, you screen so they do not have to), then offer a structure. If they hold firm and it does not pencil, walk.

*When they say no after an internal discussion:* accept it cleanly. Then leave terms "on file for whenever timing changes." Same content, completely different feel, and it keeps the relationship warm.

## 9. Honesty Guardrails

These came up constantly and they protect the business.

- If a candidate accepted another offer, say so. Do not stall.
- If the economics do not work for you, say so plainly. Clients respect it.
- If you misread their business, own it and pivot. "That was the wrong outreach on our end."
- If you do not have a reference in their vertical, admit it and reframe as flagship opportunity.
- If a claim cannot be verified (education, production numbers), build verification into your screening rather than passing it through.
- Unverifiable contract terms (like "candidate was on Indeed") should be replaced with something provable, such as documented prior contact within 30 days.

**The pattern:** honesty with a skeptic is not a weakness, it is the differentiator. Every client burned by recruiters is testing whether you will overpromise.

## 10. Discovery Questions

**Universal:**
- Which one or two seats are hurting most right now?
- What does the ideal profile look like?
- Where does your current list of open roles live?
- Who should candidates be submitted to?
- What have you tried already and why did it not work?
- What is the comp range or bill rate?
- Is this confirmed and funded, or contingent on something?

**For staffing-specific fit:**
- W2 or 1099?
- Onsite, hybrid, or remote?
- Direct hire or contract?
- Is relocation on the table?
- What is your interview and decision process?
- Do you work through a VMS?

**For talent-acquisition leaders:**
- How is your recruiting team structured?
- What percentage of hires come from third parties?
- How do you currently engage outside firms, and where has it worked or not?

## 11. Industry Pitch Angles

**Logistics and freight.** Freight sales talent is the niche. Active pipeline of 1099 and salaried reps with existing books of business. Match the sub-segment exactly: brokerage vs asset-based, flatbed, drayage, reefer, consolidation, international forwarding, air and ocean.

**Construction.** PMs, superintendents, estimators, field leadership. Match the vertical: data centers, healthcare, education, water and wastewater, modular, environmental. Public agency work (DOT, transit authorities, school construction) narrows the pool sharply because of documentation and compliance requirements. Say that out loud, it is a credibility moment.

**Manufacturing.** Technical sales and engineering-adjacent roles. Speak to CNC, precision machining, quoting accuracy, margins, quality systems.

**Automotive dealerships.** OEM franchise vs independent matters a lot. Master techs, service advisors, parts. Vetting should cover location and commute, certifications, factory diagnostic systems, tool investment, and rate expectations.

**Institutional finance.** Understand that relationships move through consultants, RFPs, and fiduciary process. "Portable book" is largely a myth here. What is portable: consultant relationships, knowledge of upcoming searches, and RFP and finals skill. Saying this earns instant credibility.

**Professional services and consulting.** Proposal assembly, knowledge management, resource utilization, client intake friction.

## 12. Pre-Call Preparation

Before any call with an unfamiliar industry:

1. **Learn how they actually make money.** What is the unit of revenue?
2. **Learn the gatekeepers.** Who decides, who influences, who blocks?
3. **Learn the vocabulary.** Enough to not sound lost, never enough to fake expertise.
4. **Know the sales cycle length.** It shapes what "urgent" means to them.
5. **Prepare five to eight discovery questions.**
6. **Know your three-line pitch:** passive headhunting, we screen not you, contingent so no risk.
7. **Identify the trap.** The one claim in your outreach that an insider would find naive.

---

## 13. Reading the Buyer

**Decision maker.** Owns budget and headcount. Push for terms and next steps.

**Gatekeeper.** HR, TA, executive assistants. Make their life easy, never go around them once identified.

**Referrer.** Points you elsewhere. Thank them, CC them once, then BCC. They are a long-term asset.

**Wrong contact entirely.** Someone in sales or operations who has no hiring authority. Do not push. Ask for the right name and get out of their inbox gracefully.

**Signals someone is not the buyer:** vague answers about process, "you would need to talk to," redirects to a generic inbox, or a title that has nothing to do with hiring.

---

## 14. When to Walk

Stop investing when:
- They say "not interested" or a management team has decided
- The role structure cannot support a viable fee and there is no larger role attached
- They are hostile or ask to be left alone
- Three or more touches with zero engagement and no research hook available
- The economics require building a custom framework for a single small placement

**How to walk:** short, gracious, door open. "Wishing you a strong rest of the year. We will be here if timing changes." Never guilt, never a final pitch.

## 15. Phrase Bank

Lines that consistently work:

- "Let me be straight with you, since that is how you operate."
- "I would rather be honest than overpromise."
- "No pressure either way, I just want to make sure I am not chasing something that is no longer on the table."
- "A clean no is genuinely more useful to me than silence."
- "If it is not the right time, no hard feelings, just tell me and I will get out of your hair."
- "We are not resume pushers."
- "You risk nothing to find out."
- "That is exactly where we earn our keep."
- "I would rather show you a real structure than hand you a vague promise."
- "Tell me what a strong hire looks like to you and I will go find that person instead of guessing."
- "Either way works, just let me know."

**The either/or close.** When you need a fast yes or no, give a menu:
1. "Let's talk" and I will call you this week
2. "Not now" and I will leave you be
3. "Talk to [name]" and I will take it there

## 16. Quick Reference: Common Situations

| Situation | Move |
|---|---|
| Prospect asks a direct question | Answer it plainly first, then add value |
| Prospect redirects to someone else | Fresh intro, referrer on CC, then BCC next round |
| Thread goes quiet 2+ weeks | Research for a hook, lead with it |
| Prospect is clearly the wrong fit | Own the misread, pivot or close gracefully |
| Prospect challenges your credibility | Answer honestly, do not bluff, reframe the gap |
| Prospect writes very short replies | Match it, use a binary menu |
| Prospect signs the agreement | Ask for priority roles and where the real list lives |
| Prospect asks for candidates | Now loop in Operations |
| Prospect says no | Accept it, leave terms on file, stay warm |
| Voicemail full or not set up | Text instead, identify yourself immediately |
