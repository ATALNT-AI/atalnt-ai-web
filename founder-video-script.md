## Recommendation: split it into two videos

Arunima's plan was 2.5–3 min of slides plus 3–3.5 min of product — call it 6 minutes. The first take ran **5:47 for the slides alone**, with no product in it at all.

Six minutes is too long for a page where most people came to see the product, not a founder and a deck. **Split it:**

| | Video | Length | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Why ATALNT** — the five slides | **~2:15** | The site, the ad's landing page, outbound |
| **B** | **See it work** — product walkthrough | **~2:45** | Same page, directly beneath. Also the thing you send when a prospect asks "what does it actually do?" |

Three reasons this beats one long video:

1. **The product video stands alone.** Half your viewers want the demo and nothing else. Right now it's buried behind two minutes of slides.
2. **You can re-record one without redoing the other.** The positioning will change before the product does.
3. **Two watchable videos beat one abandoned one.** A 6-minute video on a landing page mostly gets 90 seconds watched.

If you'd rather ship one file, run them back to back with the product starting at 2:15 — the scripts below join cleanly.

# Video A — Why ATALNT (~2:15)

Target ~150 words/minute. You naturally run ~172, so consciously slow down; the pauses between slides are where this lands. Read it once out loud before recording.

### Slide 1 — *Recruiting is changing.* · 0:00 – 0:24

> Hey — I'm Nik, co-founder and COO at ATALNT AI. That's A-T-A-L-N-T, dot A-I.
>
> For as long as I've been in recruiting, companies have had two options. Pay an agency every time you hire. Or build the whole thing yourself — recruiters, job boards, sourcing tools, enrichment, outreach, all of it.

**Why the spelling.** Loom's transcriber heard your company name seven times in the last recording and got it wrong seven times — "Talent.AI," "Atalan AI," "talent in AI," never once ATALNT. If a speech model can't hear it, a prospect can't type it. Spell it once, early, while the wordmark is on screen. Never again after that.

### Slide 2 — *Two ways to recruit.* · 0:24 – 1:00

> Here's the tradeoff.
>
> The agency model gives you people and real recruiting execution — but every hire is another fee. I've watched this play out over and over. The client's excited, they find someone great, and then they see the number and they freeze. Internally we call it **the danger zone**. Sticker shock, then second-guessing, on a twenty-five percent placement fee.

>

**The danger zone is the best line you have.** It's yours, it's specific, and it names a feeling every hiring manager has had and never had a word for. Say it slowly and let it sit.

It gives the human half a *second* beneficiary — not just the buyer's comfort, but the candidate's actual experience — and it attacks every AI-screening competitor without naming one.

# Video B — See it work (~2:45)

Screen recording, your voice over it. No slides. The bottom strip of Slide 5 is already the storyboard: **Role → Candidates → Intelligence → Engagement → Screening → Interview.**

**Open cold. No re-introduction** — you already said who you are.

### 1 · The role · 0:00 – 0:30
> Let's take a real one. Plant Manager.
>
> I'm going to paste in the job description exactly as the client sent it. It's messy — they always are. ATALNT structures it into the role, the seniority, and what actually matters, then asks me the two or three questions that change who shows up. That's the whole setup.

Show the paste. Show the mess resolving. Show one clarifying question and answer it.

### 2 · Candidates · 0:30 – 1:00
> A few days later, this. Ranked, not just collected. Every one of these came from either our internal database or AI sourcing across job boards and external data.
>
> And these aren't people who applied. That's the point — the ones you want usually aren't applying to anything.

### 3 · Intelligence · 1:00 – 1:30
> Here's where it stops being a list. Role fit, experience, skills, leadership — scored, with the reasoning behind it. So when I tell you this person's a ninety-two, I can show you why.

### 4 · Engagement · 1:30 – 2:00
> Outreach goes out across email, LinkedIn, and phone — personalized off what we actually know about them, not a mail merge.
>
> And when they reply, they're talking to a person on my team. Not a bot.

### 5 · Screening · 2:00 – 2:25
> Screening happens before anything reaches you. Skills, experience, culture, references — a real conversation, then a recommendation. You're seeing four or five people worth your time, not forty.

### 6 · Interview · 2:25 – 2:45
> And it ends here. Interview scheduled, on your calendar, confirmed with the candidate directly. You never sent a link or chased anybody.
>
> That's the whole thing — intake to scheduled interview. If you're writing agency cheques every month, let's talk. Book a walkthrough at atalnt.ai.

**End on the CTA.** The first take closed on *"I think you guys are going to love it,"* which is a hope, not an instruction. Tell them exactly what to do.

## Delivery

The first take had **51 instances of "uh"/"uhm"** in 994 words, plus 9 "you know" and 8 "I think." That's normal off-the-cuff, and reading from this script removes most of it on its own. Three habits to consciously drop:

- **"I think the slide sort of speaks for itself"** and **"I won't spend too much time here."** Both tell the viewer this bit doesn't matter. If it doesn't, cut the slide.
- **Slide fumbling on camera** — *"that's going to take me to my second slide here, uh, let's see, here we go."* Have the deck in presenter mode and advance clean.
- **Trailing off.** Several sentences in the first take ended in "and so forth" or "and everything else." Land each slide on its last word and stop. The pause is what makes it sound certain.

Record it twice. The second take is always the one.
