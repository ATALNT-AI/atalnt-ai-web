# ATALNT AI — Promo Ad (58 seconds)

**Video 1 of 2.** Paid social: Instagram + Facebook, feed and Reels. This is the production script — everything needed to shoot or generate it is in this file.

*Video 2 (the ~3:30 product demo) is drafted in [demo-script.md](demo-script.md), along with the four alternate 30-second concepts this was built from. Not needed yet.*

---

## The decision behind this cut

**One video, both buyers.** The audience splits into the HR/talent leader who signs and the line leader with an empty seat who champions. A single creative can't be title-targeted reliably, so this is engineered so both find themselves in it:

- **Pain 1 (quality)** and **Pain 2 (speed)** are felt by both — that's why they're first.
- **Pain 3** carries the line leader's exact reality: *three people covering one seat.*
- **Solution 3** carries the talent leader's scoreboard: *flat price instead of 20% per hire.*

If you later split into two campaigns, those are the two lines to swap. Everything else holds for either.

**Structure:** three pains stacked and escalating, a turn that names the enemy, then three solutions resolved *in the same order*. An unresolved problem is an open loop and open loops hold attention; resolving them in order is what makes the back half feel like payoff instead of a feature list.

**The one rule that matters most on set:** the pain half is nearly silent. On-screen text and one short line per beat — let the images do it. Wall-to-wall voiceover over the problem section is the single most common way ads like this die.

## Script

**Total runtime 0:58.** VO target ≈ 2.8 words/second — energetic but not rushed.

**Timed and verified: 141 words of voiceover ≈ 50 seconds of speech, leaving ~8 seconds of deliberate silence, all of it in the first half.** Per-section rates below stay between 1.3 and 3.1 w/s, with the slow beats deliberately placed on the pain.

| Section | Slot | Words | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain 1 | 6s | 8 | 1.3 — mostly silence, intended |
| Pain 2 | 9s | 16 | 1.8 — the held beat on the greyed card |
| Pain 3 | 9s | 24 | 2.7 |
| Turn | 6s | 9 | 1.5 — let it land |
| Solution 1 | 11s | 34 | 3.1 — the densest beat, watch it |
| Solution 2 | 7s | 20 | 2.9 |
| Solution 3 | 7s | 20 | 2.9 |
| Close | 5s | 10 | 2.0 |

If a rewrite pushes any section past ~3.2 w/s, move the line to a **text card** rather than speeding up the read. Solution 1 already does this — *"3 people you'd actually call"* is on screen, not spoken, precisely because that beat was running 4.1 w/s and the callback was too important to cut.

### PAIN 1 — Quality · 0:00 – 0:06

- **ON SCREEN:** An applicant list scrolling fast, dozens of rows blurring past. It stops hard on a counter: `412 applicants`. Then rows desaturate one by one, rapidly, until two remain lit. Text: `2 worth calling`.
- **VO:** "Four hundred and twelve applicants. Two worth calling."
- **AUDIO:** No music yet. Interface sounds only.
- **VISUAL PROMPT:** A long list of anonymous applicant rows scrolling rapidly on a clean cream dashboard, motion blur, stopping on a large counter reading 412. Then rows desaturating one by one in rapid succession until only two remain highlighted. Muted, overwhelming, no faces.

### PAIN 2 — Speed · 0:06 – 0:15

- **ON SCREEN:** One candidate card, lit, match `96%`. Cut to calendar clutter — overlapping invites, *"Does Thursday work?"*, *"Sorry, can we move it?"*, a day counter climbing `1 · 3 · 6 · 9`. Cut back to the card: greyed out, stamped `Accepted elsewhere`. **Hold a full beat in silence.**
- **VO:** "You found the right one on Tuesday. Nine days of scheduling later, she'd taken another offer."
- **AUDIO:** Still no music. The hold on the greyed card is silent.
- **VISUAL PROMPT:** A single candidate card centered on warm cream with a high gold match score, softly lit against darkness. Then overlapping email threads and calendar invites accumulating, a small day counter incrementing from one to nine. Then the same candidate card fully desaturated to grey with a small stamp reading "Accepted elsewhere," alone on a dark background. Still and final.

### PAIN 3 — Capacity · 0:15 – 0:24

- **ON SCREEN:** Pull back. The card is one row on a board of eleven open roles, day counters climbing, several tinted warm red. Under one role, three name rows appear, each labelled *covering*.
- **VO:** "And eleven more roles behind her. Three people are covering one of those seats — which is why nobody has time to fill it."
- **AUDIO:** A low tone enters underneath, barely there.
- **VISUAL PROMPT:** Camera pulling back to reveal a list of open job requisitions with climbing day counters, several rows tinted warm red. Three simple name rows appear beneath one requisition, each with a small muted label, connected by thin lines. Clean dashboard, quietly heavy, sense of compounding backlog.

### THE TURN · 0:24 – 0:30

- **ON SCREEN:** Everything clears to black. Two lines, one at a time: `Not a hiring problem.` / `A capacity problem.`
- **VO:** "That's not a hiring problem. That's a capacity problem."
- **AUDIO:** **Music enters here.** Warm, sparse, unhurried. This is the only cue in the video and the contrast is the entire effect.
- **VISUAL PROMPT:** Two lines of elegant serif text appearing in sequence on deep black with a faint warm gold glow, generous spacing. Total visual silence after clutter.

### SOLUTION 1 — Quality · 0:30 – 0:41

- **ON SCREEN:** The wordmark, briefly. Then two lines land in sequence: `AI brings the scale.` / `Humans bring the judgment.` Cut to a counter spinning up to `30+ job boards`, then cold outreach firing — email, voice, text — into a field of candidates who were never in the applicant list. Dissolve to a ranked shortlist: three candidate cards, gold match scores, skill tags. **Text card over the shortlist:** `3 people you'd actually call.`
- **VO:** "ATALNT AI. **AI brings the scale. Humans bring the judgment.** Our agents work thirty-plus job boards and cold-outreach the people who *aren't* applying. Then an in-house account manager screens every one by hand."
- **VISUAL PROMPT:** Two lines of elegant serif text appearing in sequence on deep black with warm gold glow. Then a counter rapidly incrementing to "30+", surrounded by many small labeled source nodes illuminating at once, thin gold lines reaching outward to dim silhouetted profile markers that light up as they're contacted. Dissolving into a recruiting interface on warm cream with three candidate cards, circular avatars, large gold percentage match scores and small skill tag pills.

> **Why the manifesto line sits here and not at 0:00.** This is the moment it's worth the most. Tension has been built across three pains and released by the turn — the viewer is now actually asking *"so what's the alternative?"*, and this answers it in eight words. Opening with it means saying it to someone who hasn't agreed to care yet. Same sentence, roughly ten times the value, purely from position.

> **Why "aren't applying" is the load-bearing word in this ad.** Pain 1 was 412 applicants and two worth calling. The honest answer to that pain isn't "we search better" — it's that *the people you want were never in that list.* Passive sourcing is the only thing that actually resolves the loop, and it's the one line here a job board or an ATS cannot say back to you. If anything gets cut for time, cut something else.

### SOLUTION 2 — Speed · 0:40 – 0:48

- **ON SCREEN:** The account manager card — dark avatar, gold initials, *Alex Rivera · your account manager*. An availability panel. Then one clean confirmation: `Booked — Wednesday 2:00pm`.
- **VO:** "They confirm interviews directly with the candidate. You share when you're free. No links, no chasing. No nine days."
- **VISUAL PROMPT:** A profile card with a dark circular avatar and gold initials beside a simple availability selector, resolving to a single clean booked-confirmation panel. Warm cream, gold accents, calm and immediate.

### SOLUTION 3 — Capacity & cost · 0:48 – 0:55

- **ON SCREEN:** The eleven-role board returns — now calm, neutral-toned, every row carrying a gold **Ready** badge. It collapses into one flat line: `one flat monthly price`.
- **VO:** "Do that for all eleven roles — the price doesn't move. One flat monthly fee, not twenty percent per hire."
- **VISUAL PROMPT:** A previously cluttered warm-red requisition board now calm and neutral, each row carrying a small gold "Ready" pill badge, then collapsing and merging into a single clean line of text. Relief and order.

### CLOSE · 0:55 – 0:58

- **ON SCREEN:** Two lines land in sequence: `Not AI versus people.` / `AI and people.` → ATALNT AI wordmark → `First shortlist inside a week · atalnt.ai`
- **VO:** "The future of recruiting isn't AI versus people. It's both."
- **VISUAL PROMPT:** Two short lines of elegant serif text appearing in sequence on deep near-black with a warm gold glow, dissolving into a minimal serif wordmark with a radial gold glow and a single line of small supporting text beneath.

> **Note on the close.** The proof point moved from the voiceover to the screen. The last thing said should be the line they'd repeat to a colleague; the last thing *shown* should be the reason to click. Doing both in VO makes the ending sound like a list. Total runtime goes to ~1:00 — still inside the target.

## Callbacks — don't lose these in the edit

Four lines in the second half deliberately echo the first. They're what make it feel resolved rather than merely finished. If a cutdown breaks a pair, cut *both* halves, never just one:

| Setup | Callback |
|---|---|
| "Two worth calling" | "Three people you'd **actually call**" |
| "Nine days of scheduling" | "No links, no chasing. **No nine days.**" |
| "Eleven more roles behind her" | "Do that for **all eleven roles**" |
| "**AI** brings the scale. **Humans** bring the judgment." | "Not **AI** versus **people**. It's both." |

That last pair is the frame of the whole ad — stated at 0:30 as the product's design, restated at 0:55 as what it means. It's also the one an editor is most likely to break, because the two halves sit 25 seconds apart and each reads fine alone.

## Before you shoot

**Reseed the demo data first.** The app and the site's hero shortlist are seeded with freight and logistics roles — Senior Freight Broker, Fleet Dispatcher, reefer lanes, McLeod TMS (see `components/sections/shortlist-preview.tsx`). On camera that makes you look like a logistics point solution. Swap in sector-neutral roles: Account Executive, Operations Manager, Staff Accountant, Customer Success Manager. Same for the fee ledger in `components/sections/problem.tsx`.

**Do not film the login screen.** It displays "Protected by ATALNT · SOC 2 Type II" with no qualifier. The audit is *in progress*, not complete — and a certification claim you don't hold yet is exactly what an enterprise security review catches later.

**Every number on screen is a depicted scenario, not a statistic.** 412 applicants, nine days, 91 days, eleven roles — all illustrative, the same way the site's fee ledger is. The VO never states them as fact, never cites research, and never attributes them to ATALNT results. This is the easiest thing to break in a rewrite; check it at the edit.

**"30+ job boards" is the one exception — it's a product capability claim, not a scenario.** It needs to be literally true and countable, because it's specific enough that a prospect will ask on the first call. Confirm the actual number before this ships. If it's 34, "30+" is right; if it's 28, change the line rather than the count.

**Also don't claim:** "2.4M profiles indexed" (pending substantiation in `lib/site.ts`), any named client or testimonial, or anything implying the client's talent team gets replaced.

### New product facts folded into this cut

Three things worth stating precisely, because they're sharper than what the site currently says:

| Fact | How it's used |
|---|---|
| Sourcing agents work **30+ job boards** | Replaces the vague "live postings." Concrete beats broad |
| Cold outreach targets **passive candidates** — people not applying | The actual resolution to Pain 1. Load-bearing |
| The account manager is **in-house** and understands the model | Reinforces the site's "not a rotating offshore queue" |

**Worth flagging back to the website:** `lib/site.ts` lists `SOURCING_CHANNELS` as seven generic buckets, one of which is just "Job Postings." That undersells 30+ boards, and it hides the passive-candidate capability entirely behind "Email / Voice / Text Campaigns" — which reads as spam tooling rather than as the answer to the applicant-quality problem. The site is currently making a weaker version of the argument this ad makes.

## Production notes

**If a person is making this:**
- Screen capture at 2× / Retina, scale down. Full-width browser, no bookmarks bar, notifications off.
- Record VO separately and cut picture to voice. Every timing above is a target.
- Slow every UI interaction to about half your natural speed. Sluggish on set reads as confident on playback.
- **Cut the music down in the pain half, not up.** The instinct is a driving track under the problem. Do the opposite — strip it to near-silence and bring the music in on the turn at 0:24. That contrast is doing more work than any single line of copy.

**If an AI tool is generating this:**
- Feed one scene at a time. The VISUAL PROMPT lines stand alone with no other context.
- Prepend to every prompt: *warm cream and near-black palette, gold accents, elegant serif headlines, generous white space, soft raised shadows, editorial and premium, no stock-photo people.*
- Never let a generator produce UI text — it renders garbled words. Generate backgrounds and composite real captures or real typography over them.

**Music:** sparse, warm, unhurried. Nothing driving. The product's argument is that it makes hiring calm — a frantic track contradicts the pitch.
